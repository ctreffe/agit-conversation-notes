# DDR-0003: Explain specialist context through clarification footnotes

Status: Accepted
Date: 2026-10-13
Complements: [DDR-0002](DDR-0002-content-led-provisional-notes.md)

## Context

Transcript clarification also elicits compact explanations of specialist terms,
internal structures and background. The maintainer wants this knowledge retained
without lengthening the main narrative, improving personal recall and readability
for readers who lack that background.

## Decision

Select useful explanations from clarification answers and condense them into
short footnotes at the first relevant term or passage. Retain substantive results,
arguments and important uncertainty in the main text. Source footnotes continue
to provide actual transcript locators; explanatory notes identify supplementary
clarification. Combine the two functions only when the note stays readable.

Keep a private link to the relevant clarification answer. Preserve qualifiers
instead of turning remembered or tentative background into established facts.
Spelling corrections and full question/answer rounds need no automatic footnotes.

## Rationale

This makes internal knowledge reusable while keeping the content-led account
readable and preserving the distinction between conversation evidence and later
explanation.

## Consequences

The drafting skill, generic starter and workflow guidance support both source
and explanatory notes. Actual explanations and their provenance remain unversioned
under PDR-0002. The current personal-recall audience and sharing boundaries remain
unchanged. Evaluate usefulness and density at the existing first/second-trial
template checkpoint.

## Alternatives considered

- Put every explanation in the main narrative: distracts from discussion content.
- Append the entire clarification exchange: obscures compact reference knowledge.
- Treat added explanations as original transcript statements: loses provenance.

## Follow-up

Exercise the footnote model in the first draft and DOCX review; refine it using
maintainer feedback rather than introducing a mandatory glossary or fixed layout.
